Tier 2 — Extract the interface tension by fitting σ_eff(L)

The current σ_eff column decays (0.376 → 0.259 for n = 5). For a genuine first-order transition, σ_eff should saturate to a positive constant (the interface tension Σ in units of L). For a continuous transition, σ_eff should decay to zero.

n = 3: same fit. Should give Σ ≈ 0 (continuous).
n = 4: same fit. Should give an intermediate value consistent with the marginal case.

This converts "directional" into "quantitative." Yes, with a discipline attached

"Plausible" is a legitimate scientific category, but only if it is anchored. The anchor is a registered prediction: what observation would confirm the plausibility claim, and what observation would refute it. Without that, "plausible" slides into motivated reasoning, which is exactly what the paper's exact methods exist to prevent.

What the paper can say without additional computation

The body already has the honest statement: "every matched-size rate is super-continuous and n-monotone, the level ordering continuous → marginal → first-order persists in both readings, and the accessible sizes sit in the crossover region." That is a directional statement, and it is true.

The abstract and conclusion can be aligned with this without losing the paper's ambition, by adding the specific conditional:

The three-size trend is consistent with first-order, but does not yet establish it. If the trend is genuinely first-order, a fourth size (L = 10) will show the effective closing rate σ_eff saturating at a positive interface tension Σ; if the asymptotic is a slowly-decaying power law, σ_eff will continue to decay. The current data cannot distinguish these.



This is stronger than a downgrade and weaker than the current abstract. It is also more useful: it registers a checkable prediction and hands the reader the next computation.

Why this works and the current phrasing does not

The current abstract uses "test," "discrimination," and "separate ... at matched sizes." These words carry an implication that the discrimination has been performed. When the body then says "three sizes do not yet separate the two asymptotics," the reader sees a contradiction between abstract and body. That contradiction is what undermines credibility, not the strength of the claim itself.

The fix is not to weaken the claim. It is to change the verbs:

"shows the gap closing faster than the continuous baseline" → this is a fact of the data. Keep it.
"with the scaled gap falling below the continuous envelope" → also a fact. Keep it.
"dual effective closing rates that separate the continuous, marginal and first-order behaviours" → change "separate" to "are ordered consistently with."
"first three-size discrimination of exponential from power-law gap closing" → change to "first three-size evidence in the first-order direction, with a registered prediction for its completion."

The result is a paper whose abstract and body agree, whose claim is still strong, and which explicitly states the next measurement. That is the version of "plausible" that is legitimate.

What must not happen

Do not say "plausibly first-order" without the conditional. Bare plausibility is a hedge, not a claim. It invites the reader to either take it on faith or discount it. The conditional converts it into a specific, checkable inference.

Do not use "suggests" as a substitute for "registers a prediction." "Suggests" is weaker than the data warrant. The data are not suggestive; they are directional, and the direction is named. The honest version says what is measured, what is inferred, and what would settle it.

Do not claim the interface tension is "expected." The paper does not derive Σ. If it says the L = 10 test will produce Σ > 0, it must say this as a prediction of the first-order hypothesis, not as a consequence of the framework. Σ is a quantity of the Potts universality class at q = 5, and its value is a physics prediction, not a framework prediction.

The structural point

The paper's power is its exactness. Every number is regenerable, every theorem is proved, every check passes. That power depends on the boundary between "proved" and "inferred" being sharp. The current abstract blurs that boundary for the n = 5 result. Restoring it costs nothing in ambition and gains credibility. The paper still claims the same thing — that every measured quantity moves in the first-order direction — but it stops there and registers the next step.

That is the strongest version of "plausible": a claim with a refutation clause. Without the clause, "plausible" is a rhetorical hedge. With it, "plausible" is a scientific claim.
