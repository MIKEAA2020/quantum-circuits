Growth-Rate Regularity for the Three-Replica Transfer Operator

Theorem (Growth-rate regularity for n = 3)

Let $d \ge 2$, $p \in (0,1)$, and let $\Ccomp^{(3)}(m,p)$ be the compressed three-replica transfer operator at size $m = L/2$, defined by Eq. (3.14) with the bond weights of Eq. (3.20) and the Gram structure of Theorem 3.6. Let $\lambda_1(m,p)$ be its leading (Perron) eigenvalue, and let $f_m(p) = m^{-1}\log\lambda_1(m,p)$. Then:

(i) The limit $\Lambda(p) = \lim_{m\to\infty} f_m(p)$ exists and is finite for every $p \in (0,1)$, and is independent of the boundary conditions.

(ii) $\Lambda(p)$ is real-analytic on $(0,1)\setminus D$, where $D$ is the set of points at which the gap between $\lambda_1(m,p)$ and the subleading eigenvalue closes as $m\to\infty$.

(iii) The convergence $f_m \to \Lambda$ is uniform on compact subsets of $(0,1)\setminus D$.

(iv) For $n=3$, $d=2$, the numerical evidence of Sec. 4.4 (Table 4.2) identifies $D = {p_c^{(3)}}$ with $p_c^{(3)} = 0.305(3)$.


Proof

Step 0: Setup and the Gram structure

Write $V_m = (\mathbb C^{6})^{\otimes m}$ for the bond-label space (at $d=2$ the physical subspace has dimension $5^m$ after removing the sign-representation linear dependence, but we work in the ambient $6^m$-dimensional space; the Gram matrix $G_m = G^{\otimes m}$ is positive semidefinite with a known kernel, and all statements below hold on the physical quotient). The compressed operator is

$$
\Ccomp^{(3)}(m) = M_1(m),M_2(m),
$$
with $M_1, M_2$ defined by Eq. (3.14):
$$
(M_1){\sigma'\sigma} = \prod{k=1}^m w(\sigma'k \mid \sigma_k,\sigma{k+1}),\qquad
(M_2){\sigma'\sigma} = \prod{k=1}^m w(\sigma'k \mid \sigma{k-1},\sigma_k),
$$
indices mod $m$, and
$$
w(\tau \mid \mu,\nu) = \sum_{\rho\in S_3} \Wg_{4}(\tau^{-1}\rho),
\bigl[(1-p),2^{c(\rho^{-1}\mu)} + 2p\bigr],
\bigl[(1-p),2^{c(\rho^{-1}\nu)} + 2p\bigr].
$$

By Theorem 3.6(i), $\Ccomp^{(3)}$ is self-adjoint and positive semidefinite in the Gram inner product $\langle x,y\rangle_{G_m} = x^{\mathsf T} G_m y$:
$$
G_m,\Ccomp^{(3)}(m) = \bigl(\Ccomp^{(3)}(m)\bigr)^{\mathsf T} G_m \succeq 0.
$$
Equivalently, with $S(m) = G_m^{1/2} M_1(m) G_m^{-1/2}$,
$$
\Ccomp^{(3)}(m) \sim S(m) S(m)^{\mathsf T},
$$
and $\lambda_1(m,p) = |S(m)|^2$, the square of the leading singular value of $S(m)$ in the standard inner product.

The bond weights $w(\tau\mid\mu,\nu)$ are polynomials in $p$ with rational coefficients, of degree $2$ in $p$ (each factor is linear in $p$, and there are two factors per bond). Hence $\Ccomp^{(3)}(m,p)$ is a polynomial in $p$ of degree $2m$.


Step 1: Finite-size analyticity

For each fixed $m$, $\Ccomp^{(3)}(m,p)$ is a polynomial in $p$, hence an analytic family of matrices on $\mathbb C$. The leading eigenvalue $\lambda_1(m,p)$ is simple for every $p\in(0,1)$. Indeed, $S(m)S(m)^{\mathsf T}$ is positive semidefinite, and its leading eigenvalue is simple iff the leading singular value of $S(m)$ is simple, which holds iff $S(m)$ is irreducible in the sense that its leading left and right singular vectors are unique. The bond weights connect all bond labels for $p\in(0,1)$ (the weight matrix $w(\tau\mid\mu,\nu)$ has full support on $S_3^3$ for $p\in(0,1)$, since both the measurement term $2^{c(\rho^{-1}\mu)}$ and the gate term $2p$ are strictly positive, and the Weingarten function $\Wg_4$ is nonzero on all of $S_3$). Hence $S(m)$ is irreducible, and $\lambda_1(m,p)$ is simple.

By the Kato–Rellich theorem (Kato, Perturbation Theory for Linear Operators, Ch. II, §6), a simple eigenvalue of an analytic family of matrices is analytic in the parameter. Therefore

$$
f_m(p) = \frac{1}{m}\log\lambda_1(m,p)
$$
is analytic on $(0,1)$ for every $m$.


Step 2: Subadditivity inequality

Let $m = m_1 + m_2$. Split the bond labels as $\sigma = (\sigma_L,\sigma_R)$ with $\sigma_L \in S_3^{m_1}$, $\sigma_R \in S_3^{m_2}$. The half-transfer operators factorize as

$$
M_1(m_1+m_2) = M_1(m_1)\otimes M_1(m_2) + B_1,\qquad
M_2(m_1+m_2) = M_2(m_1)\otimes M_2(m_2) + B_2,
$$
where $B_1,B_2$ are boundary terms acting only on the two bonds adjacent to the interface between the two subsystems (the last bond of the left subsystem and the first bond of the right subsystem). Because $w$ depends on three consecutive bonds, the interface involves exactly two bonds from each subsystem, and the boundary operators have the form
$$
B_1 = \sum_{\text{interface configs}} \bigl[w(\tau'\mid\mu,\nu) - w(\tau'_L\mid\mu_L,\nu_L)w(\tau'_R\mid\mu_R,\nu_R)\bigr],|\tau'\rangle\langle\sigma|,
$$
with the sum running over $O(1)$ interface configurations. Each term is a polynomial in $p$ of degree $2$, and the number of terms is finite (bounded by $|S_3|^4 = 1296$). Hence
$$
|B_1| \le C_b(p),\qquad |B_2| \le C_b(p),
$$
where $C_b(p)$ is a polynomial in $p$ of degree $2$, hence bounded on compact subsets of $(0,1)$.

Let $x_1$ be the Perron vector of $\Ccomp^{(3)}(m_1)$ and $x_2$ the Perron vector of $\Ccomp^{(3)}(m_2)$, normalized in the $G$-inner product: $\langle x_i,x_i\rangle_{G_{m_i}} = 1$. Define $x = x_1\otimes x_2 \in V_{m_1+m_2}$. Then

$$
\langle x, \Ccomp^{(3)}(m_1+m_2),x\rangle_{G_{m_1+m_2}}
= \langle x_1,\Ccomp^{(3)}(m_1)x_1\rangle_{G_{m_1}}\langle x_2,\Ccomp^{(3)}(m_2)x_2\rangle_{G_{m_2}}

\langle x, (B_1M_2 + M_1B_2 + B_1B_2),x\rangle_{G_{m_1+m_2}}.
$$

The first term equals $\lambda_1(m_1)\lambda_1(m_2)$. The boundary term is bounded in absolute value by $C'(p) = 2C_b(p)|M_2| + C_b(p)^2$, where $|M_2| \le |M_2|_{\mathrm{HS}}$ is bounded on compact subsets of $(0,1)$ because the bond weights are bounded. Therefore

$$
\langle x, \Ccomp^{(3)}(m_1+m_2),x\rangle_{G_{m_1+m_2}}
\ge \lambda_1(m_1)\lambda_1(m_2) - C'(p).
$$

Since $\lambda_1(m_1+m_2) \ge \langle x, \Ccomp^{(3)}(m_1+m_2)x\rangle_{G}/\langle x,x\rangle_{G}$ and $\langle x,x\rangle_G = 1$, we obtain the lower bound

$$
\lambda_1(m_1+m_2) \ge \lambda_1(m_1)\lambda_1(m_2) - C'(p).
$$
Equivalently, since $\lambda_1(m) \ge \lambda_1(1)^m > 0$ (the single-bond eigenvalue is positive and bounded below on compact sets),
$$
\log\lambda_1(m_1+m_2) \ge \log\lambda_1(m_1) + \log\lambda_1(m_2) - C''(p),
$$
with $C''(p) = -\log(1 - C'(p)/\lambda_1(m_1)\lambda_1(m_2))$ bounded on compact subsets of $(0,1)$ (for $p\in(0,1)$, $\lambda_1(m_1)\lambda_1(m_2)$ is bounded away from zero and $C'(p)$ is bounded, so $C''(p)$ is finite and bounded).

For the upper bound, use the fact that the Perron eigenvalue of the combined system is at most the product of the Perron eigenvalues plus a boundary correction. The standard argument: for any positive operator $A$ on $V_{m_1}\otimes V_{m_2}$ that is a bounded perturbation of $A_1\otimes A_2$, one has $\lambda_1(A) \le \lambda_1(A_1)\lambda_1(A_2) + |A - A_1\otimes A_2|$. Applying this to $A = \Ccomp^{(3)}(m_1+m_2)$ and $A_1\otimes A_2 = \Ccomp^{(3)}(m_1)\otimes \Ccomp^{(3)}(m_2)$ (which is positive in the $G$-inner product) gives
$$
\lambda_1(m_1+m_2) \le \lambda_1(m_1)\lambda_1(m_2) + C'(p),
$$
hence
$$
\log\lambda_1(m_1+m_2) \le \log\lambda_1(m_1) + \log\lambda_1(m_2) + C''(p).
$$

Combining both bounds:
$$
\bigl|\log\lambda_1(m_1+m_2) - \log\lambda_1(m_1) - \log\lambda_1(m_2)\bigr| \le C''(p).
$$


Step 3: Existence of the limit

The two-sided inequality of Step 2 is the standard almost-subadditivity condition. Fekete's lemma in its two-sided form states: if a sequence $(a_m)$ satisfies $|a_{m_1+m_2} - a_{m_1} - a_{m_2}| \le C$ for all $m_1,m_2$, then the limit $\lim_{m\to\infty} a_m/m$ exists and is finite.

The proof is elementary. For any $m,n$, write $m = qn + r$ with $0 \le r < n$. Iterating the inequality,
$$
a_m \ge q,a_n + a_r - qC,\qquad a_m \le q,a_n + a_r + qC.
$$
Dividing by $m = qn + r$:
$$
\frac{a_n}{n} - \frac{C}{n} + O(1/m) \le \frac{a_m}{m} \le \frac{a_n}{n} + \frac{C}{n} + O(1/m).
$$
Taking $m\to\infty$ with $n$ fixed, then $n\to\infty$, gives
$$
\limsup_{m\to\infty}\frac{a_m}{m} \le \liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{a_n}{n},\qquad
\liminf_{m\to\infty}\frac{a_m}{m} \ge \limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{a_n}{n},
$$
so the limit exists and equals $\lim_{n\to\infty} a_n/n$.

Applying this to $a_m = \log\lambda_1(m,p)$ gives the existence of $\Lambda(p) = \lim_{m\to\infty} f_m(p)$ for every $p\in(0,1)$. The limit is finite because $0 < \lambda_1(m,p) \le |M_1(m)||M_2(m)| \le (\max_{\tau,\mu,\nu}|w(\tau\mid\mu,\nu)|)^{2m} < \infty$.

The limit is independent of boundary conditions: different boundary conditions (open, twisted, etc.) modify only $O(1)$ bonds at the boundary, giving a perturbation of norm $O(1)$, which contributes $O(1/m)$ to $f_m$ and vanishes in the limit.


Step 4: Uniform convergence

The constant $C''(p)$ in the almost-subadditivity inequality depends on $p$ only through the bond weights $w(\tau\mid\mu,\nu)$, which are polynomials in $p$. On a compact subset $K \subset (0,1)$, the bond weights are bounded and bounded away from zero, so $C''(p)$ is bounded by a constant $C_K$ depending only on $K$. The Fekete argument then gives
$$
|f_m(p) - \Lambda(p)| \le \frac{C_K}{m} + O(1/m^2)
$$
for all $p \in K$. This is uniform convergence on $K$.


Step 5: Analyticity of the limit

By Step 1, each $f_m$ is analytic on $(0,1)$. By Step 4, $f_m \to \Lambda$ uniformly on compact subsets of $(0,1)\setminus D$, where $D$ is the set of points at which the gap $\lambda_1(m,p) - \lambda_2(m,p)$ fails to be bounded below uniformly in $m$. By Weierstrass's theorem (uniform limit of analytic functions is analytic), $\Lambda$ is analytic on $(0,1)\setminus D$.

The set $D$ is discrete. Indeed, the gap function $g_m(p) = \lambda_1(m,p) - \lambda_2(m,p)$ is analytic in $p$ for each $m$, and its limit $g(p) = \lim_{m\to\infty} g_m(p)$ is analytic on $(0,1)\setminus D$ by the same uniform-convergence argument. The zero set of a non-identically-zero analytic function is discrete. The points of $D$ are precisely the accumulation points of the zero set of $g$, hence isolated. (If $g$ were identically zero on an interval, the leading eigenvalue would be degenerate on that interval, which would require the bond weights to satisfy a nontrivial algebraic identity for all $p$; the numerical evidence at $n=3$, $d=2$ shows a single transition, so this does not occur.)

The numerical evidence of Sec. 4.4 (Table 4.2) shows:

The correct proof

There is no complete proof of growth-rate regularity for n=3. What can be proven is a partial result for $p > (3-\sqrt3)/2 \approx 0.634$, using the multiplicative Fekete argument with entrywise positive weights. Below that threshold — including at $p_c^{(3)} = 0.305$ — no proof is currently available. The manuscript's open-problem list is correct; the failed attempt was an overreach the manuscript itself does not make. Here is what actually works.

The correct partial result

Theorem. Let $d=2$, $n=3$, and $p \in ((3-\sqrt3)/2, 1)$. Then the limit
$$
\Lambda(p) = \lim_{m\to\infty} \frac{1}{m}\log\lambda_1(m,p)
$$
exists and is finite, is continuous on this interval, and the convergence is uniform on compact subsets with rate $O(1/m)$.

Proof.

Step 1 (positivity in the open interval). By the review's check, all 216 bond weights $w(\tau\mid\mu,\nu)$ are strictly positive for $p > (3-\sqrt3)/2$. Let
$$
R(p) = \frac{\max_{\tau,\mu,\nu} w(\tau\mid\mu,\nu)}{\min_{\tau,\mu,\nu} w(\tau\mid\mu,\nu)} \in (1,\infty).
$$
$R(p)$ is finite and continuous on this open interval, and blows up only as $p \downarrow (3-\sqrt3)/2$ (where a weight vanishes) or $p \uparrow 1$ (where $R \to 1$).

Step 2 (multiplicative Fekete bound). Fix $m_1, m_2 \ge 1$, $m = m_1 + m_2$. Write $\sigma = (\sigma_L, \sigma_R) \in S_3^{m_1} \times S_3^{m_2}$. The bond-weight product for $M_1(m)$ factorizes as
$$
(M_1(m)){\sigma'\sigma} = \Bigl[\prod{k=1}^{m_1-1} w(\sigma'{L,k}\mid\sigma{L,k},\sigma_{L,k+1})\Bigr] w(\sigma'{L,m_1}\mid\sigma{L,m_1},\sigma_{R,1}) \times \Bigl[\prod_{k=1}^{m_2-1} w(\sigma'{R,k}\mid\sigma{R,k},\sigma_{R,k+1})\Bigr] w(\sigma'{R,m_2}\mid\sigma{R,m_2},\sigma_{L,1}),
$$
with the two boundary factors $w(\sigma'{L,m_1}\mid\sigma{L,m_1},\sigma_{R,1})$ and $w(\sigma'{R,m_2}\mid\sigma{R,m_2},\sigma_{L,1})$ replacing the cyclic boundary conditions $w(\sigma'{L,m_1}\mid\sigma{L,m_1},\sigma_{L,1})$ and $w(\sigma'{R,m_2}\mid\sigma{R,m_2},\sigma_{R,1})$ of the two smaller rings. The ratio is
$$
\frac{(M_1(m)){\sigma'\sigma}}{(M_1(m_1)\otimes M_1(m_2)){\sigma'\sigma}} = \frac{w(\sigma'{L,m_1}\mid\sigma{L,m_1},\sigma_{R,1}), w(\sigma'{R,m_2}\mid\sigma{R,m_2},\sigma_{L,1})}{w(\sigma'{L,m_1}\mid\sigma{L,m_1},\sigma_{L,1}), w(\sigma'{R,m_2}\mid\sigma{R,m_2},\sigma_{R,1})} \in [R^{-2}, R^2].
$$
Hence, entrywise,
$$
R^{-2}, M_1(m_1)\otimes M_1(m_2) \le M_1(m) \le R^{2}, M_1(m_1)\otimes M_1(m_2),
$$
and identically for $M_2$.

Step 3 (transfer to $C_{\mathrm{comp}}$). Since all matrices are entrywise nonnegative, the product inequality propagates:
$$
R^{-4}, C_{\mathrm{comp}}(m_1)\otimes C_{\mathrm{comp}}(m_2) \le C_{\mathrm{comp}}(m) \le R^{4}, C_{\mathrm{comp}}(m_1)\otimes C_{\mathrm{comp}}(m_2).
$$
For entrywise nonnegative matrices, $\rho(A\otimes B) = \rho(A)\rho(B)$, and $A \le B$ entrywise implies $\rho(A) \le \rho(B)$ (Perron–Frobenius monotonicity). Therefore
$$
R^{-4},\lambda_1(m_1)\lambda_1(m_2) \le \lambda_1(m) \le R^{4},\lambda_1(m_1)\lambda_1(m_2),
$$
i.e. with $a_m = \log\lambda_1(m,p)$,
$$
|a_{m_1+m_2} - a_{m_1} - a_{m_2}| \le 4\log R(p).
$$

Step 4 (Fekete). Write $m = qn + r$, $0 \le r < n$. Iterating the bound $q$ times:
$$
a_m \le q,a_n + a_r + 4q\log R, \qquad a_m \ge q,a_n + a_r - 4q\log R.
$$
Dividing by $m = qn + r$ and letting $q \to \infty$ with $n$ fixed gives
$$
\Bigl|\frac{a_n}{n} - \limsup_{m\to\infty} \frac{a_m}{m}\Bigr| \le \frac{4\log R}{n}, \qquad \Bigl|\frac{a_n}{n} - \liminf_{m\to\infty} \frac{a_m}{m}\Bigr| \le \frac{4\log R}{n}.
$$
Taking $n \to \infty$, the limsup and liminf coincide, so $\Lambda(p) = \lim_m a_m/m$ exists and satisfies $|\Lambda(p) - a_n/n| \le 4\log R(p)/n$ for every $n$ — uniform $O(1/n)$ convergence.

Step 5 (continuity). Each $a_n(p)/n$ is continuous on the open interval (finite sum of continuous functions; the leading eigenvalue is simple for all $p \in (0,1)$ by Theorem 5(i) of the manuscript, or by the strict contraction bound). The $O(1/n)$ bound is uniform on compact subsets, so $\Lambda = \lim_n a_n/n$ is continuous. $\square$

At $p > 0.634$, Perron–Frobenius gives simplicity because $C_{\mathrm{comp}}(m)$ is entrywise positive and irreducible. Below $0.634$, simplicity is a numerical observation, not a theorem.

The gap below $p = 0.634$, and why the obvious fixes fail

Why the measurement-pattern expansion does not close it. Expanding $M_p^{(n)} = (1-p)\mathrm{id} + p\mathcal{D}n$ site by site and pushing through the Haar twirls gives
$$
\Phi_n(p) = \sum{S,T \subseteq [L]} (1-p)^{2L-|S|-|T|} p^{|S|+|T|}, \mathcal{E}_e \mathcal{D}_n(S) \mathcal{E}_o \mathcal{D}_n(T),
$$
with all coefficients positive for $p \in (0,1)$. Each term is a positive operator in the Gram inner product (Theorem 5(i)), so $\Phi_n(p)$ is a sum of positive operators with positive coefficients — hence a positive operator, which is already Theorem 5(i). But this is operator positivity, not entrywise positivity in the bond basis. The bond basis ${U_\sigma\otimes U_\sigma}$ is orthogonal in the Gram inner product, not in the standard one; its matrix elements of a positive operator can have either sign. The Fekete argument needs entrywise positivity, and the measurement-pattern expansion does not supply it.

Why analytic continuation from the positive region fails. The Fekete bound has constant $4\log R(p)$, and $R(p) \to \infty$ as $p \downarrow (3-\sqrt3)/2$. The bound degenerates at the endpoint and cannot be continued analytically. Analytic continuation of the conclusion (existence of the limit) from a region where it is proven to a larger region would require knowing a priori that the limit function has an analytic extension — which is what we are trying to prove.

Why the Gram-adjoint structure alone is insufficient. $C_{\mathrm{comp}} \sim S S^{\mathsf T}$ with $S = G^{1/2}M_1G^{-1/2}$ real. This gives $\lambda_1 = |S|^2$, and one might try to bound $|S(m_1+m_2)|$ in terms of $|S(m_1)||S(m_2)|$. But $S$ is not entrywise nonnegative even when $M_1$ is, and singular values do not satisfy a Fekete-type subadditivity in general. The boundary term in the factorization $S(m) = S(m_1)\otimes S(m_2) + B$ has norm comparable to the bulk (each factor carries the same exponential growth), so the triangle inequality gives $|S(m)| \le |S(m_1)||S(m_2)| + |B|$ with $|B|$ not small relative to the bulk.

What a complete proof would require

One of the following, none of which is currently available for $n=3$:

A positive-weight representation of the $n=3$ transfer matrix in a suitable basis — for instance a Fortuin–Kasteleyn cluster expansion, or a representation in terms of a positive-temperature Potts model. If the empirical $q=n$-Potts identification could be made exact (a Temperley–Lieb or FK equivalence for $n \ge 3$), positivity would follow immediately and the Fekete argument would extend to all $p \in (0,1)$. This is the most promising route and is exactly what the Potts-universality conjecture suggests should exist.


A Lee–Yang type theorem for the complex zeros of $\det(\lambda I - C_{\mathrm{comp}}(m,p))$, uniform in $m$, in a complex neighborhood of the real $p$-interval. Such theorems exist for ferromagnetic Ising and related models (Lee–Yang, Newman, Lieb–Sokal); whether the $n=3$ replica model falls into that class is open.


A cluster expansion valid on the whole interval $(0,1)$, not just near $p=1$. The $p=1$ expansion works because the weights become $\mu,\nu$-independent; extending the convergence region to the critical point would require controlling the complex zeros of the partition function uniformly in $m$ — equivalent to (2).


Direct control of the boundary term in the multiplicative Fekete argument without positivity — for instance, a proof that the boundary operator $B$ in $M_1(m) = M_1(m_1)\otimes M_1(m_2) + B$ has norm bounded by $C(p)$ times the bulk, with $C(p)$ finite on $(0,1)$. This would require a structural theorem about the $n=3$ bond channel that is not presently known.

Corrected theorem statement

Theorem (partial growth-rate regularity for $n=3$). Let $d=2$, $n=3$, $p \in ((3-\sqrt3)/2, 1)$. Then $\Lambda(p) = \lim_{m\to\infty} m^{-1}\log\lambda_1(m,p)$ exists, is finite, is continuous, and the convergence is uniform on compact subsets with rate $O(1/m)$. $\Lambda(p)$ is real-analytic in a neighborhood of $p=1$.

Conjecture. $\Lambda(p)$ exists, is finite, and is real-analytic on $(0,1)\setminus{p_c^{(3)}}$, with $p_c^{(3)} = 0.305(3)$ the three-state-Potts transition. This is proven for $n=2$ by the exact Ising solution (Proposition 4 of the manuscript) and is open for $n \ge 3$.



Bottom line

The correct proof is the multiplicative Fekete argument applied to entrywise positive bond weights, valid for $p > (3-\sqrt3)/2 \approx 0.634$. It gives existence, finiteness, continuity, and $O(1/m)$ convergence — but not analyticity, and nothing below $0.634$. The manuscript's open-problem list is correct: growth-rate regularity for $n \ge 3$ at $p < 0.634$ (including $p_c^{(3)}$) is not proven. The most promising route to a complete proof is a positive-weight representation via a Fortuin–Kasteleyn or Temperley–Lieb equivalence, which the empirical $q=n$-Potts identification suggests should exist but which has not been exhibited for $n \ge 3$.
