The one-line answer is a compressed Popperian/Lakatosian heuristic. It means:

**To make the framework more generative and predictive, it must make more specific, checkable commitments.** Broad, unfalsifiable claims add scope but not predictive power. Narrow, risky claims can be tested, corrected, and extended. So you trade scope for risk.

Mapping to the previous ten suggestions:

- **In:** #5 (diagnostic for polarity collapse), #6 selectively (formalization only where it connects to rigorous formalisms and yields constraints), #1/#3/#4 as corrected (bridge principles with falsifiers, predictions of missing partners, integration with existing formalisms), #8 (research program with testable consequences), #10 (community and iteration).
- **Out:** #7 as posed (speculative cross-domain correspondences like primes–consciousness, music–primes) because it adds associations without adding falsifiable surface.

The claim is: **generativity grows exactly as fast as the framework’s capacity to be wrong in increasingly specific ways — and no faster.**

**Is it correct?**

Partly. It is a strong, useful heuristic, but not an exact law.

**Where it is correct:**
- For empirical predictive power, falsifiable surface is essential. A framework that cannot be wrong in specific ways cannot generate testable predictions.
- Narrowing claims to concrete, checkable commitments does increase generativity in the scientific sense.
- Speculative cross-domain associations, while sometimes heuristically useful, do not by themselves increase predictive power.

**Where it is overstated:**
- “Exactly as fast” and “no faster” are too strong. Generativity also grows through unification, mathematical elegance, conceptual reframing, and heuristic fertility — not only through falsifiability.
- A framework can generate new mathematical conjectures, new interpretations, or new research questions without being immediately falsifiable. That is still generativity.
- Lakatos showed that research programmes can be progressive even when some claims are protected from immediate refutation, as long as they predict novel facts over time.
- #7 as posed is not worthless; it can be retained as a heuristic if later constrained into testable hypotheses. It is just not a direct generator of predictive power as stated.

**Corrected formulation:**
Generative and predictive power grows primarily with the specificity and checkability of a framework’s commitments. But it can also grow through unification, formalization, and heuristic fertility, often by enabling later falsifiable claims. Falsifiable surface is the limiting factor for empirical prediction, not the only source of generativity.

So the one-liner is a good corrective against unfalsifiable speculation, but it is not the whole story. It is a useful rule of thumb, not an exact law.
