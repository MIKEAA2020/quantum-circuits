- \(

# 2. Replace the verbal polarity template with a formal conjecture calculus

## Why this is second

v8 improves P4 by distinguishing duality, complementarity, and \(Z_2\)-grading. But RP1 and FT1 still largely treat them as one generic template, with adjunction as the presumed universal form.

That remains too permissive. Typed slots prevent some category mistakes, but they do not yet generate constraints. A template becomes generative only when:

> **Given some slots, it restricts the missing slot to a small candidate set before the answer is known.**

If all four slots are supplied retrospectively, the result is taxonomy, not prediction.

## Formalize the species separately

### A. Exact duality

Require:

- theories or categories \(\mathcal C,\mathcal D\);
- an equivalence \(F:\mathcal C\to\mathcal D\), with inverse up to natural isomorphism;
- a parameter map \(\phi:\Lambda_{\mathcal C}\to\Lambda_{\mathcal D}\);
- observable functors satisfying a commuting relation such as

\[
O_{\mathcal D}\circ F \cong O_{\mathcal C}.
\]

The output must be a missing dictionary entry, parameter transformation, or observable equality.

### B. \(Z_2\)-grading or involution

Require:

\[
\tau:X\to X,\qquad \tau^2=\mathrm{id},
\]

with a decomposition into sectors and a specified invariant

\[
I(\tau x)=I(x).
\]

The output must be a missing partner, selection rule, forbidden transition, or degeneracy relation.

### C. Complementarity

Require two observables or subalgebras with a precise incompatibility condition—for example noncommutation or an uncertainty bound. “Two different descriptions” is not enough.

The output must be a quantitative trade-off or invariant state-level constraint.

### D. Perspective-cut

Model a cut as a projection, channel, or subalgebra—not merely as verbal inclusion/exclusion. A family of cuts may have a multidimensional moduli space; it need not possess one scalar “parameter.” The generated claim concerns what remains invariant when the cut moves.

## Demote adjunction from universal center

An adjunction is not generally an exact duality, and its unit/counit are not naturally “the parameter.” FT1 should become:

> **Adjunction is one admissible asymmetric correspondence species, not the formalization of polarity as such.**

Exact equivalence, involution, complementary algebras, projections, and adjunctions need separate admissibility rules.

Likewise, Noether should not be the criterion for every row. It applies to continuous symmetries of dynamical systems, not automatically to dualities, complementarity, or discrete involutions.

## Turn the calculus into an actual generator

For each prospective entry:

1. Fix the species.
2. Supply typed known data.
3. Withhold one slot.
4. Generate a finite ranked list for the missing slot.
5. Freeze the list publicly.
6. Compare it with an independent derivation or experiment.

A valid yield has the form:

> “Given identity \(X\), transformation class \(T\), and parameter space \(\Lambda\), the framework restricts the invariant or partner to \(I_1,I_2,I_3\).”

A row where the investigator supplies identity, modes, parameter, and invariant after the fact remains **recognition-only**.

## Make one narrow problem the flagship

The 2+1-dimensional particle–vortex duality program is the better initial vehicle, but only if W3 names something concrete, such as:

- a previously unsettled boundary-condition map;
- a parameter transformation;
- an anomaly-matching condition;
- an observable or transport-coefficient equality.

The pre-registration must identify exactly which relation is being predicted and what numerical or formal result would contradict it.

By contrast, the current **A3-swampland flagship is too broad**. “Every boundary is theorem-shaped” is a research conviction, not yet a discriminating prediction; its falsifier—an accepted selector not reducible to consistency—is too elastic. Retain it as a long-range commitment until it forbids a specific EFT class that mainstream consistency reasoning has not already excluded.

## Benchmark and kill condition

Compare the polarity calculus against:

- ordinary symmetry reasoning;
- standard duality heuristics;
- domain-expert forecasts without the framework;
- designated negative controls.

Kill or demote it if, over a predeclared series of problems, it:

- produces no better top-\(k\) candidate set than those baselines;
- requires retrospective reinterpretation of its slots;
- succeeds only where the duality or invariant was already assumed;
- cannot generate a missing relation before it is independently known.

Success means at least one independently confirmed constraint that:

1. was prospectively generated;
2. was not imported through the premises;
3. is useful to researchers who reject Part I.

# Upgrade 2 — Run a blind retrodiction benchmark: the framework's first real data point

## Why second

Upgrade 1 makes bets that can lose. The next question is whether the method generates anything better than ordinary physics heuristics. Right now there is **no evidence either way**: RP3's E-rows are all recognitions after the fact.

Waiting for prospective results (Upgrade 3) takes years. A retrodiction benchmark can produce a first number within months.

## Design

**Case set: about 10 historical problems with known resolutions.** Mix in cases where the template *should* help with cases where it should not:

- *Expected to favor the template:* antiparticle prediction before 1931; electric–magnetic duality in gauge theory before Montonen–Olive (1977); non-abelian dualities before Seiberg (1994); gauge/gravity correspondence before 1997.
- *Expected to fail (controls):* three generations; CKM matrix values; the Higgs mass; the neutrino mass ordering.

**Protocol:**

1. For each case, freeze a literature snapshot dated before the resolution.
2. Two teams work under identical constraints:
   - **Team T** applies the seven-step method plus the inversion rule.
   - **Team B** applies standard symmetry and naturalness reasoning without the template.
3. Each team outputs a **finite ranked candidate set**, top-k with k ≤ 5, for the withheld answer.
4. Scorers check whether the true answer appears and at what rank.

**Contamination control:** this is the hard part, since everyone knows the outcomes. Two mitigations:

- Use participants from outside the subfield, with literature restricted to the snapshot.
- Or, more cleanly, require that both teams' outputs be *mechanically derivable* from snapshot inputs plus the declared method, then audited by a third party.

**Scoring:** hit rate at top-k, mean rank, and performance on the control cases (where the template should *not* help). If the template "succeeds" on the controls, that is strong evidence its slot-filling is retrospective.

## Value

This answers the question the Delivery Charter can't currently answer: **does the template beat the baseline at all?**

- **Positive result:** the first non-circular evidence of heuristic power. It belongs in W5 and gives external researchers a reason to try the method.
- **Null result:** the contraction clause should trigger early, before more effort goes into it.

Either outcome is more informative than anything in v1–v8.

**Kill/demotion rule (pre-registered):** if Team T does not outperform Team B on hit rate at top-k across the non-control cases, RP1 loses "generator" status and is retagged as interpretive.

# 2. Make The "Invariant" The Demanding Element Of The Template

> **Second Highest Impact**

Your definition is:

> **D4 — Polarity.** A structure is polar iff it has **one identity, two modes, a parameter, and an invariant.**

Of those four elements, **the invariant is by far the most underused.**

Scan RP3 and you will notice something consistent: many entries have plausible polar pairs and plausible parameters, but their **invariants** are very weak. 

Examples of this are

- *"Stability / Novelty — invariant: identity through change"* (qualitative)
- *"Dynamics / Statics — invariant: equations of motion"* (this is circular. The equations of motion *describe* the dynamics, they are not obviously the invariant preserved across the dynamics/statics polarity)
- *"Yin/Yang — invariant: the Tao (still)"* (metaphorical)
- *"Time: Chronos/Kairos — composition of temporalities"* (already flagged as prose-invariant)

The problem is this: **If you can write almost any vague phrase and call it "the invariant", the template becomes trivial to apply to anything.**

But in actual mathematics and physics, **invariants are restrictive.**

Noether's theorem is powerful precisely because *finding the symmetry/invariant pair is non-trivial.* Group invariants, characters, conserved charges, central charges, topological invariants, $L$-functions — these are **mathematical objects** with precise properties. They constrain.

**The Upgrade:**

Replace the loose demand *"Demand the invariant."* (RP1, step 4) with a stricter criterion:

> **Step 4 (Revised) — Demand a structural invariant.**  
> The invariant must be something that **remains the same when you vary the parameter, or when you map between the two modes.** More strongly: *there must exist a transformation induced by moving along that parameter (or relating the two modes) under which this object is preserved.*

Also add this test to every RP3 entry:

> **Invariant Test:** *If I change the parameter, does this invariant remain unchanged? If I map Mode 1 <-> Mode 2, what is preserved?*

If you cannot answer those two questions clearly, **the entry does not satisfy D4 yet.**

**Why this is #2:**

Right now, the framework mostly asks: *"What are the two sides?"* 

It needs to ask far more often: **"What is it that refuses to change across those two sides?"**

**The moment you start taking that question seriously, the template stops being a descriptive lens and starts becoming a filtering tool.** Many proposed polarities in RP3 would fail this test and either need revision, or be downgraded to illustrative.

# 3. Invert The Method: Start From Open Problems, Not Established Structures

> **Third Highest Impact**

This is the one that directly addresses RP3b's emptiness.

Look at how RP3 is built. Almost every row reads the same way:

> *Take some established domain. Notice a duality/opposition. Fit identity/modes/parameter/invariant to it.*

That is **backward application**.

RP3b correctly states the remedy: *Backward-looking corpora do not count as generativity. Every entry requires an **open problem**, stated before consulting the dominant solution.*

But v8 states this as a rule for projections. **It should be made into a general method of the research program itself.**

**The Upgrade:**

Introduce what I would call the **Inverse Template Method.**

Instead of starting with

> *"Find a polarity in this theory."*

Start with

> ***"This theory has an open problem. What polarity would, if correctly identified, help explain or constrain it?"***

Formally, operationalize it like this:

**Inverse Seven-Step Method**

1. **Pick an open problem.** State it specifically. Prefer problems where physicists disagree, or where multiple approaches coexist.
2. **Ask what must remain invariant.** Given this problem, what structural feature ought to be preserved if the theory is unified/coherent?
3. **Ask what two regimes are being treated separately.** Many unification problems arise because we are holding two descriptions apart that should be seen as modes of one thing.
4. **Hypothesize the identity.** What single structure unifies those regimes?
5. **Identify the parameter of limitation.** What parameter distinguishes them?
6. **State the structural invariant explicitly.** (Using the test from #2)
7. **Derive a constraint.** *If this polarity is correct, what must be true of this open problem that is not obvious from either regime alone?*

**Then, only then, check whether this constraint is already known.** If it is already known, it is recognition. **If it is not known, or if it rules out a class of approaches, it becomes a candidate for RP3b.**

**Why this is #3:**

The current method naturally produces **post-hoc fits**. The inverse method naturally produces **pre-hoc candidates**.

The 2+1D particle–vortex web chosen for W3 is a step in this direction. But rather than reserving this for a single flagship, **the framework should make this the default mode of work.**

If, over the next year, the program applies this inverse method to even **3–5 genuinely open problems** and pre-registers the resulting constraints in RP3b, you will immediately know far more about whether this framework has generative power than by adding another dozen entries to RP3.
