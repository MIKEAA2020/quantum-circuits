## General relativity

- **Ontology:** Spacetime geometry is the dynamical entity. Matter tells spacetime how to curve; spacetime tells matter how to move.
- **Key equation:** Einstein field equations,

$$G_{\mu
u} + \Lambda g_{\mu
u} = \frac{8\pi G}{c^4} T_{\mu
u}$$

- **Symmetry:** Diffeomorphism invariance — the laws are the same under arbitrary smooth coordinate transformations. There is no preferred background.
- **Time:** Dynamical. Time is not an external parameter but part of the gravitational field. The theory is **background-independent**.
- **Locality:** Strictly local. The field equations are differential equations at a point.

## Quantum theory

- **Ontology:** States in a Hilbert space; operators representing observables; probabilities given by the Born rule.
- **Key structure:** The algebra of observables; unitary evolution; measurement.
- **Symmetry:** Unitary symmetry; gauge invariance (in QFT).
- **Time:** An external parameter. The Schrödinger equation $i\hbar \partial_t |\psi\rangle = H|\psi\rangle$ presupposes a background time.
- **Nonlocality:** Entanglement is fundamentally nonlocal. Bell's theorem shows that no local hidden-variable theory reproduces quantum predictions.

## The structural opposition

| Feature | GR | QM/QFT | Polarity type |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spacetime | Dynamical, background-independent | Fixed background, non-dynamical | Identity / mode |
| Time | Part of the system | External parameter | Mode switch |
| Locality | Strictly local | Fundamentally nonlocal | Conjugacy-complementarity |
| Degrees of freedom | Geometry (metric tensor) | Matter fields (operators) | Identity / mode |
| Continuum | Smooth manifold | Potentially discrete at short distances | UV/IR polarity |
| Information | Encoded in geometry | Encoded in quantum states | Two modes of one encoding? |

Framework reading:

> These are not two unrelated theories that happen to describe overlapping domains. They are **two modes of one identity** — two aspects of a single underlying structure that appear irreconcilable only because each theory has been developed from one side of a polarity that has not yet been recognized as a polarity.

The task of quantum gravity, under the framework, is:

$$\boxed{\text{Find the identity of which GR and QM are two modes. Name the parameter and the invariant.}}$$

# 3. The five deep polarities of quantum gravity

The point–circle grammar does not yield a specific Lagrangian. It identifies the **structural polarities** that any successful quantum gravity must resolve, and it specifies what "resolution" means in each case: finding the identity, the parameter, and the invariant.

## Polarity 1: Geometry / Algebra

**The polarity:**

General relativity is geometric: the fundamental object is a smooth manifold with a metric $g_{\mu
u}$, and physics is described by curvature, geodesics, and causal structure.

Quantum theory is algebraic: the fundamental object is an algebra of observables (operators on a Hilbert space, or a $C^*$-algebra, or a von Neumann algebra), and physics is described by states, expectation values, and commutation relations.

| Slot | Content |
|---|---|
| Identity | One quantum-gravitational structure |
| Mode 1 | Geometry (smooth manifold, metric, curvature) |
| Mode 2 | Algebra (operators, Hilbert space, $C^*$-algebra) |
| Parameter | Energy/scale: at low energies, geometry dominates; at high energies, algebraic structure dominates |
| Invariant | The physical content that both descriptions encode — observables, transition amplitudes, scattering data, entropy |

**The point–circle reading:**

At large $r$ (low energy, long distance), the display is geometric: spacetime is smooth, the metric is well-defined, causal structure is clear. This is the fully articulated circle.

At small $r$ (high energy, Planck scale), the geometric description degenerates — singularities appear, the metric fluctuates, causal order may become ambiguous. The algebraic description must take over: commutation relations, operator algebras, quantum information.

The identity is what remains the same under the transition from geometric to algebraic description. Candidates:

- **Spectral triples** (Connes): a triple $(\mathcal{A}, \mathcal{H}, D)$ where $\mathcal{A}$ is an algebra, $\mathcal{H}$ is a Hilbert space, and $D$ is a Dirac operator. Geometry is *reconstructed* from algebraic data. The Connes–Chamseddine spectral triple for the Standard Model is a concrete instance.

- **Algebraic quantum field theory (AQFT):** local algebras of observables associated to spacetime regions, with the spacetime geometry entering through the causal structure that constrains which algebras commute. The geometry is implicit in the algebraic relations.

- **Holographic dualities:** a boundary algebraic theory (CFT) encodes a bulk geometric theory (gravity). The exact dictionary between them *is* the identity.

**What the framework predicts (regulative):**

Any successful quantum gravity must present a **precise, invertible dictionary** between the geometric and algebraic descriptions. A theory that offers only one side — pure geometry without algebraic structure, or pure algebra without geometric reconstruction — has not found the identity. It has mistaken one mode for the whole.

This is the framework's **duality-hunting heuristic (H3)** applied with full force.

## Polarity 2: Background-independence / Background-dependence

**The polarity:**

GR is background-independent: there is no fixed spacetime stage on which physics happens. The stage itself is dynamical. Diffeomorphism invariance means that points of the manifold have no intrinsic physical identity — only relational, diffeomorphism-invariant quantities are physical.

QFT is background-dependent: it is formulated on a fixed spacetime (usually Minkowski space or a fixed curved background). The Hilbert space, the vacuum, the particle content — all depend on the background.

| Slot | Content |
|---|---|
| Identity | One quantum-gravitational theory |
| Mode 1 | Background-independent (no fixed stage; diffeomorphism-invariant) |
| Mode 2 | Background-dependent (fixed stage; Fock space, particles, vacuum defined relative to it) |
| Parameter | Scale / energy: at low energies, a background emerges and QFT works; at Planck energies, no fixed background exists |
| Invariant | Diff-invariant observables, relational quantities, scattering amplitudes that do not depend on a choice of background |

**The point–circle reading:**

The background is the circle: a fixed, articulated structure (spacetime) that provides the stage for physics. Background-independence is the point: no fixed structure, no preferred stage, only relations.

At large $r$, the circle is stable: a background spacetime emerges (approximately), QFT is well-defined on it, particles can be defined. At small $r$, the background fluctuates, the circle wobbles, and the stage itself becomes part of the physics.

The resolution is not to choose one side. It is to find the theory in which a background **emerges** from a background-independent structure at large $r$, while at small $r$ the background-independent description is primary.

This is precisely the point–circle transition:

$$\text{background-independent identity (point)} \;\xrightarrow{\;r\text{ large}\;}\; \text{background-dependent effective description (circle)}$$

**Candidates:**

- **Loop quantum gravity (LQG):** directly background-independent. Spacetime is built from spin networks (combinatorial/algebraic structures). A smooth geometry emerges in an appropriate limit. The parameter $r$ here is related to the spin labels and area/volume eigenvalues.

- **Causal dynamical triangulations (CDT):** builds spacetime from discrete simplicial building blocks with a causal structure. No fixed background. The dimensionality and geometry emerge dynamically. The parameter $r$ is the number of simplices / the linear extent.

- **Causal sets:** spacetime is a locally finite partially ordered set. The continuum is an approximation. The parameter $r$ is the density of events.

- **String theory on AdS backgrounds:** technically background-dependent (expansion around a fixed background), but the AdS/CFT correspondence provides a non-perturbative, background-independent *definition* of the theory via the boundary CFT.

**What the framework predicts (regulative):**

The polarity between background-independence and background-dependence is a genuine conjugacy-complementarity (P4 species). No theory that is purely one or the other at all scales is complete. The successful theory will show that background-independence and background-dependence are **two descriptions of the same structure at different values of $r$**.

## Polarity 3: Locality / Nonlocality

**The polarity:**

GR is local: the Einstein equations are differential equations at a point. The equivalence principle demands that physics looks the same in a freely falling frame at any spacetime point.

QM is nonlocal: entanglement correlations violate Bell inequalities. A measurement on one particle instantaneously constrains the state of a distant particle, in a way that cannot be explained by any local hidden-variable model.

| Slot | Content |
|---|---|
| Identity | One quantum-gravitational structure |
| Mode 1 | Local (pointwise field equations, equivalence principle) |
| Mode 2 | Nonlocal (entanglement, Bell correlations, holographic encoding) |
| Parameter | Scale: at large distances, effective locality holds; at small distances or across horizons, nonlocality becomes essential |
| Invariant | Causal structure (what can influence what); information-theoretic constraints (holographic bounds, monogamy of entanglement) |

**The point–circle reading:**

Locality is a circle-property: it requires extended space, distinct points, the ability to say "here" and "there." It requires the display to be articulated enough for spatial separation to be meaningful.

Nonlocality is a point-property: it signals that the underlying structure does not have the spatial separation that the circle provides. Entanglement is the display's way of revealing that the point (the undivided identity) is still present beneath the articulated circle.

The parameter $r$ determines the balance:

- At large $r$: spatial separation is well-defined, effective locality holds, field theories work.
- At small $r$: spatial separation becomes ambiguous, nonlocality dominates, the point reasserts itself through entanglement and holographic constraints.

**The profound implication:**

This is where the framework's grammar becomes most physically specific. If entanglement is the residue of the undivided point showing through the circle, then:

$$\boxed{\text{Entanglement is not a feature of quantum gravity. Quantum gravity is a feature of entanglement.}}$$

This is not a mystical claim. It is a specific research hypothesis: that spacetime geometry is **built from** entanglement structure, not the other way around. This is exactly the direction of the ER=EPR program (Maldacena and Susskind) and the tensor network / quantum error correction approaches to holography.

**Candidates:**

- **ER=EPR:** Einstein–Rosen bridges (wormholes) are the geometric manifestation of Einstein–Podolsky–Rosen entanglement. The identity is a quantum-gravitational connectivity structure. The parameter is the amount of entanglement (bond dimension in tensor network language). The invariant is the entanglement entropy / mutual information.

- **Tensor networks / MERA:** the bulk geometry is reconstructed from a network of entangled quantum degrees of freedom. The geometry *is* the entanglement pattern. Disentangling removes geometry; increasing entanglement creates it.

- **Quantum error correction:** the bulk (geometry) is encoded in the boundary (algebra) via a quantum error-correcting code. Local bulk operators are reconstructed from nonlocal boundary operators. The code is the identity; bulk/boundary are the modes; the entanglement structure is the parameter; the code distance / recovery fidelity is the invariant.

Any quantum gravity theory that treats spacetime as fundamental and entanglement as derived has the polarity backwards. ## Polarity 4: Timelessness / Temporality

**The polarity:**

The Wheeler–DeWitt equation of canonical quantum gravity:

$$\hat{H}|\Psi\rangle = 0$$

is timeless. There is no external time parameter. The quantum state of the universe satisfies a constraint equation, not an evolution equation.

Yet all of physics as we experience and measure it is temporal. Observations happen *in time*. Correlations are *between events at different times*. The second law of thermodynamics defines a temporal arrow.

| Slot | Content |
|---|---|
| Identity | One quantum-gravitational structure |
| Mode 1 | Timeless (Wheeler–DeWitt constraint; block universe) |
| Mode 2 | Temporal (experienced time; measured correlations; thermodynamic arrow) |
| Parameter | Perspective/localization (D12): from *within* the system, time appears; from the viewpoint of the whole, it does not |
| Invariant | The constraint $\hat{H}|\Psi\rangle = 0$; relational observables that are defined relative to physical clocks within the system |

**The point–circle reading:**

This is Corollary 5.1 in its most literal physical form:

$$\text{time} = \text{timelessness unfolded}$$

The point is the timeless constraint surface — the space of solutions to $\hat{H}|\Psi\rangle = 0$. The circle is the emergent temporal description that arises when one part of the system is used as a clock for another.

The parameter $r$ is the degree of localization: how much of the system has been "read off" by an internal observer using internal degrees of freedom as a reference.

**Candidates:**

- **Page–Wootters mechanism:** A subsystem $C$ serves as a clock. The conditional state of the rest of the system, given a reading of $C$, evolves in the "time" registered by $C$. Time is relational, not fundamental.

- **Tomita–Takesaki modular theory / thermal time hypothesis (Connes–Rovelli):** Given a von Neumann algebra $\mathcal{M}$ and a state $\omega$, the Tomita–Takesaki theorem produces a one-parameter group of automorphisms $\sigma_t^\omega$. This is the "flow of time" induced by the state. Change the state, change the flow. Time is not a background; it is a *derived* property of the algebra-state pair.

  In framework language:
  - The algebra $\mathcal{M}$ is the identity.
  - The state $\omega$ is the perspective (D12).
  - The modular flow $\sigma_t^\omega$ is the emergent temporal structure.
  - The KMS condition (the thermal property of the modular flow) is the invariant.

- **Causal set theory:** discrete events with a partial order. The order relation is fundamental; the continuum time parameter is emergent and approximate.

**What the framework predicts (regulative):**

The successful quantum gravity theory will be fundamentally timeless. Time will emerge from the theory's internal structure — from relations, perspectives, states — not from an external parameter. This is a direct consequence of the framework's Corollary 5.1 and P12.

## Polarity 5: Continuum / Discrete

**The polarity:**

GR uses a smooth manifold: spacetime is a continuum. Fields are smooth functions. Calculus applies.

Many quantum gravity approaches suggest that at the Planck scale, spacetime is discrete: spin networks (LQG), causal sets, triangulations, pixels of geometry.

| Slot | Content |
|---|---|
| Identity | One quantum-gravitational structure |
| Mode 1 | Continuum (smooth manifold, differential geometry, calculus) |
| Mode 2 | Discrete (graphs, sets, simplicial complexes, quanta of area/volume) |
| Parameter | Scale $r$: at large scales, the continuum approximation is excellent; at the Planck scale, discreteness appears |
| Invariant | The physical content encoded in both descriptions: correlation functions, spectra, scattering amplitudes, entropy |

**The point–circle reading:**

A circle (as a geometric object) is a continuum. But it can be described by a finite number of Fourier modes. The continuum and the discrete are not two different realities; they are two descriptions of one structure, related by a transform.

At large $r$, the continuum description is smooth and the discrete structure is invisible (just as a table looks continuous despite being made of atoms). At small $r$, the discrete structure becomes essential — the Fourier modes become sparse, the "pixels" of geometry become significant.

The deeper point–circle insight:

$$\text{continuity} = \text{discreteness at a scale where the individual units are not resolved}$$

This is not a claim that spacetime is "really" discrete. It is a claim that the continuum/discrete polarity is an instance of the limitation parameter: the same structure appears continuous or discrete depending on $r$.

**What the framework predicts (regulative):**

The correct quantum gravity will not choose between continuum and discrete. It will present a structure from which **both descriptions emerge at appropriate scales**, with a precise relationship between them (analogous to the relationship between a function and its Fourier transform, or a manifold and its triangulation). The invariant will be something that both descriptions compute consistently.
