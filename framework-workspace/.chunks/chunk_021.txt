## So What Should You Believe?

If you want the **most empirically adequate** view of the physical world: **process-relational realism** is the best bet. The world looks dynamic, relational, and emergent. Time may not be fundamental, but it is not merely an illusion either. It is real at the level of the manifest order.

If you want the **most logically simple and non-dual** view: **timeless Absolute, process as appearance**. This preserves immutability, non-duality, and completeness. But it cannot explain why process appears at all without introducing a mystery.

If you want the **most honest integration**: **two levels**. The Absolute is timeless. The manifest world is processual. Process is real as appearance, not as ultimate ground. From the Absolute’s view, no change. From within the world, real change. Both are true, but at different levels.

That is not a cop-out. It is the most defensible position given what we actually know.

**Bottom line:**

From a purely objective God’s-eye view, we do not know whether there is change. If God is timeless, no. If God is temporal, yes. If there is no God, we don’t know whether time is fundamental. Physics has not decided. Metaphysics has not decided. The honest position is **fallibilism**: process is real for us, and whether it is real in itself remains an open question—perhaps the deepest open question there is.
