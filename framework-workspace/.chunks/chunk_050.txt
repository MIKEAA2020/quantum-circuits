Yes — a framework in which process leads to eventual perfection **can make sense**, but only if you adjust one of the two key terms: _process_ or _perfection_. If you keep both in their strongest classical senses, they contradict. If you redefine one, the framework can be coherent.

Here is the logical tension:

- **Classical perfection** = no lack, no potential, no change, complete, timeless.

- **Process** = change, time, potential, becoming, lack, incompleteness.

- **Eventual** = a point in time.


So: if perfection is timeless and complete, it cannot be _reached_ at some time. If process is ultimately real, it cannot _stop_ at perfection without ceasing to be process. If it stops, then process was not ultimate. If it continues, perfection was not final.

Therefore, a process that leads to eventual perfection is coherent only if you do one of the following:

* * *

### 1\. Redefine perfection as dynamic or relational

Instead of “no change, no lack,” perfection becomes “maximal richness, harmony, creativity, love, self-expression.” This is the process view.

- **Whitehead:** reality is creativity; God grows in richness and responsiveness. Perfection is not a final state but an ideal of intensity and harmony. There is no eventual static endpoint.

- **Aurobindo:** the Absolute involves itself in matter and evolves back to itself as Supermind. Perfection is both origin and goal, but it is dynamic self-realization.

- **Teilhard de Chardin:** evolution converges on the Omega Point, a personal center of love. Perfection is a future attractor, but it is also already present as the lure.


In these views, process is real, and perfection is dynamic. The framework makes sense. But it is no longer classical perfection.

* * *

### 2\. Redefine perfection as the whole process

This is the Hegelian move. Perfection is not a final state _after_ process. It is the **totality** of the process, comprehended as a whole. The Absolute is the unity of becoming and being. Time is a moment within the whole, not the ultimate frame.

- History is the self-realization of Spirit.

- The end is not a temporal endpoint but the logical completion of the whole.

- Perfection includes change as a sublated moment.


This is coherent within absolute idealism. But perfection is not unchanging; it is the complete dialectical whole.

* * *

### 3\. Keep classical perfection, but make process subordinate

This is the two-level view. Perfection is eternal, timeless, complete. Process is the temporal appearance or expression of that perfection. The world evolves, but its evolution is not ultimately real; it is the way the timeless perfection appears from within time.

- **Aurobindo:** Brahman is perfect and unchanging; the world is its self-expression.

- **Teilhard:** Omega
