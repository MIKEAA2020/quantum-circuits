**Short answer:**

**No — not in the strongest sense, and not within your framework.**

An evolving reality cannot _become_ perfect, because perfection excludes change, lack, and time. It can only _appear_ to evolve toward a perfection that is already its ground. * * *

### Why an evolving reality cannot reach perfection

1. **Perfection = no lack, no potential, no change.**

If something is perfect, it lacks nothing. It has no potential to become something else. Change would mean gaining or losing something — which contradicts perfection.

2. **Evolution = change, time, potential, lack.**

To evolve is to move from one state to another. That implies the earlier state was incomplete. So an evolving reality is, by definition, not yet perfect.

3. **If reality eventually reaches perfection, then perfection is a future state.**

That means:

   - It was absent before.

   - It depends on a process.

   - It is contingent on time and conditions.

   - It can be lost or changed after.

     All of these contradict perfection.
4. **If perfection is reached, process must stop.**

If process continues, perfection is not final. If process stops, then process was not ultimate. Either way, you cannot have both real evolution and real perfection.

5. **“Eventually” is a time-word.**

Perfection is timeless. To say perfection is reached _at some time_ is a category error. A timeless perfection cannot be an endpoint in time.


So the logic is:

> Perfect → unchanging → no becoming → cannot be reached by evolution.
>
> Evolving → changing → lacking → cannot be perfect.

They are mutually exclusive.

* * *

### What you _can_ say within your framework

You can say:

- **The Absolute is always already perfect.** It never evolves, never reaches, never changes.

- **Appearance** — the world of time, process, and evolution — is the Absolute’s self-display. Within that display, there appears to be evolution, growth, and even a movement toward perfection.

- **But that movement is not ultimately real.** It is like a dream character striving to become the dreamer. The dreamer was never imperfect. The realization is not a change in the dreamer; it is the dissolution of ignorance in the dream.


So at the level of appearance, you can have a **soteriological narrative**: ignorance → seeking → realization. But that is not the Absolute becoming perfect. It is the Absolute _appearing_ as a process of realizing what it always was.
