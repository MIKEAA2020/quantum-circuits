I’ll be blunt, because you asked for the honest truth.

**No one knows the final metaphysics of the physical world.**

Physics gives us mathematical models that predict and control phenomena with extraordinary precision. It does not tell us what reality _is_ in itself. Metaphysics is underdetermined by evidence: many incompatible frameworks can fit the same data. So any claim to have the one accurate metaphysical description is, at best, a well-reasoned bet—not a proven fact.

That said, we can say some things with reasonable confidence about the **physical world** as we encounter it.

### What the physical world actually looks like

Modern physics does not describe a world of independent, static substances. It describes:

- **Relationality:** Quantum entanglement, field theory, and relativity all suggest that things are defined by their relations, not by intrinsic standalone properties.

- **Process:** Particles are excitations of fields; fields fluctuate; time is woven into space; events are primary, not things.

- **Emergence:** Complex structures—atoms, molecules, life, minds—arise from simpler interactions and have real causal efficacy at their own level.

- **Non-locality and holism:** Quantum correlations defy simple local causation.

- **Probability and indeterminacy:** At bottom, nature is not a deterministic clockwork.

- **Consciousness:** It exists, and it is somehow tied to physical organization, but how is unresolved.


If you ask, “What metaphysics best fits _that_?” the answer is not a static, relationless Absolute. The physical world, as described by science, looks far more like a **process-relational** reality: a dynamic network of events, relations, and emergent levels.

### Where your framework fits

Your non-dual Absolute—timeless, spaceless, relationless, unchanging, pure awareness—is a powerful and coherent description of **the ultimate ground**. It is not a description of the physical world’s structure. If you identify the physical world with the Absolute directly, you have to call its structure _appearance_ or _maya_. That is a legitimate metaphysical stance (idealism, Advaita, etc.), but it is not derived from physics, and it cannot be tested by physics. It is a realization or intuition about the ground, not an empirical description of the display.

So the honest truth is:

- **For the physical world:** process-relational metaphysics is the most empirically adequate. Whitehead and Langan are both in that family. Langan is more formal and computational; Whitehead is more experiential and organic. Both are speculative, but they fit the dynamic, relational, emergent character of nature far better than substance dualism or static monism.

- **For the ultimate ground:** your non-dual Absolute is a serious candidate. It is simple, logically tight, and existentially profound. But it does not explain the specific structure of the physical world; it explains why there is something rather than nothing, and what consciousness ultimately is.


### The honest synthesis

You can have both—but only with two levels:

1. **Ultimate:** non-dual, timeless awareness. No process, no relation, no change.

2. **Manifest/physical:** the dynamic, relational, emergent process. Real as appearance, not as ultimate.


In that view, **emergence and circular dependence are real at the manifest level**, not at the ultimate level. The physical world is the Absolute’s self-appearance as process. The circle is in the display, not in the ground.

If you insist that the circular dependence is _ultimately real_, then you have left strict non-dualism and joined process metaphysics. That’s fine—but you can’t have both fully.
