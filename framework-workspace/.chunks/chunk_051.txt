### What does _not_ work

A framework that says:

- Process is ultimately real, _and_

- Perfection is static, complete, unchanging, _and_

- Perfection is reached at some future time.


That is contradictory. You cannot have all three.

### Bottom line

Yes, a framework with process leading to eventual perfection can make sense. But it requires a choice:

- **Dynamic perfection:** process is real, perfection is open-ended or self-surpassing.

- **Holistic perfection:** perfection is the whole process, not a final state.

- **Two-level perfection:** perfection is eternal, process is its temporal appearance.


Each is logically coherent. None is forced by logic alone. The coherence depends on how you define perfection and whether you make process ultimate or subordinate. If you want both real process and classical perfection, you need a two-level metaphysics. Otherwise, you must redefine perfection.
