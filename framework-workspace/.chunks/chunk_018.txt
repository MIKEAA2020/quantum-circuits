Yes—but only if you mean **apparent** or **descriptive** circular dependence, not **real, ontological** mutual dependence.

Here’s the difference.

### If it’s real circular dependence, it breaks your framework.

Your Absolute is:

- self-existent

- non-dual

- relationless

- unchanging

- complete

- without lack


If the Absolute and appearance **really depend on each other**, then:

- The Absolute needs appearance to be itself → it lacks something without it.

- There are two things relating → non-duality is broken.

- There is a relation → relationlessness is broken.

- There is conditioning → self-existence is broken.

- There is process → immutability is broken.


So a **real** circle between Absolute and appearance turns your view into a dual-aspect process philosophy—like Langan or Whitehead—not strict non-dualism.

### If it’s apparent circularity, it fits fine.

You can say:

- Appearance depends on the Absolute.

- The Absolute does **not** depend on appearance.

- The circle is only in the way we **describe** things from inside appearance.

- Ultimately, there is no second thing, so no real relation and no real circle.


This is the classic two-truths move:

- **Ultimate truth:** only the Absolute is.

- **Conventional truth:** appearance, emergence, time, causality, and even “circular dependence” are valid ways of speaking within the display.


Like a dream:

- The dream depends on the dreamer.

- The dreamer does not depend on the dream.

- The dreamer does not change when the dream changes.

- Emergence happens in the dream, not in the dreamer.

- You can talk about causes and circles inside the dream, but they don’t touch the dreamer.


Or the rope-snake analogy:

- A rope appears as a snake.

- The snake depends on the rope.

- The rope does not depend on the snake.

- The snake’s “emergence” is apparent, not ultimately real.


### So what can you incorporate?

You can incorporate **emergence as a display-level phenomenon**:

- It’s real as appearance.

- It’s not real as ultimate fact.

- It’s how the Absolute appears from within the appearance.

- It does not introduce change, relation, or lack into the Absolute.


Your “virtuous circularity” stays **inside the Absolute’s self-identity**—not between Absolute and appearance. The Absolute knows itself by being itself. Appearance is not a second thing it needs.

### If you want real circular dependence, you’re choosing a different framework.

- **Langan:** one self-simulating reality with processor and display in feedback. Real process. Real circularity.

- **Whitehead:** creativity, many actual occasions, God dipolar. Real process. Real relationality.


Both give you richer emergence, but they sacrifice strict immutability, relationlessness, and non-duality.

### Bottom line

Yes, you can say “emergence is circular dependence of Absolute and appearance” **as a conventional or pedagogical statement**. But ultimately, it’s one-way: appearance depends on Absolute; Absolute depends on nothing. The circle is in the story, not in the storyteller.

If you make the circle real, you’ve left strict non-dualism and entered process metaphysics. You can’t have both fully. You can have both **as two levels**: Absolute as ground, process as appearance. That’s probably the cleanest way to keep your working assumption intact.
