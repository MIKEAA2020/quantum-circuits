Those two lines sound like they contradict each other, but in Whitehead they don’t. They answer two different questions.

**1\. “The deepest thing is creativity.”**

This is about the **ultimate principle**. Whitehead doesn’t think the universe is ultimately made of matter or mind. He thinks it’s ultimately made of **process**—things happening, new events arising, many becoming one, and novelty emerging. “Creativity” is his name for that ultimate fact. It is not a thing, not a mind, not a force. It’s more like the general rule that reality is always becoming. It’s the “that” of process.

**2\. “Experience is the most basic stuff.”**

This is about the **basic concrete realities**. Whitehead says the real building blocks of the universe are not dead particles. They are **actual occasions**—tiny momentary events. And each of those events has an inside. It takes in what came before it and responds. That taking-in and responding is what Whitehead means by “experience” in a very broad sense. So experience isn’t a substance; it’s the intrinsic nature of each actual occasion. It’s the “what” of concrete events.

So:

- **Creativity** = the ultimate principle of becoming.

- **Experience** = the intrinsic character of the concrete events that become.


One is abstract, one is concrete. One is the process, one is what the process is made of. No contradiction.

A simple analogy: think of a river. The deepest principle is **flow**—the fact that water keeps moving and new patterns arise. The basic concrete realities are the **water events**—each moment of water taking shape. Flow isn’t a drop of water, and a drop isn’t flow. But a river is both. Whitehead’s universe is like that: creativity is the flow; actual occasions are the drops, and each drop has an inside—experience.

Also important: Whitehead is **not** saying rocks or chairs are conscious. Only actual occasions have experience. Aggregates—like brains, rocks, or tables—don’t have a single unified experience unless they are organized in the right way. Human consciousness is a high-grade, integrated unity of many such occasions. So “experience is basic” doesn’t mean everything is conscious; it means nothing is completely dead.

God, in Whitehead, is an actual entity too—not creativity itself. God has a timeless side (all pure possibilities) and a changing side (feeling and responding to the world). But creativity is the more ultimate principle of process.

In your non-dual view, both creativity and experience-events would be part of the dynamic appearance—maya or shakti—while the Absolute remains the still, timeless awareness in which they appear. But that’s your frame, not Whitehead’s. For Whitehead, process and experience are as real as it gets.
