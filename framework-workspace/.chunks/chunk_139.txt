### 🧬 What Fractal Cosmology Says

Fractal cosmology is a set of minority theories proposing that the distribution of matter in the universe, or the structure of spacetime itself, is **fractal** across a wide range of scales [-57](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractal_cosmology). A fractal is a pattern that is **self-similar**: its structure repeats at different scales, like a coastline, a fern, or the Mandelbrot set.

Key claims from the field include:

- **Galactic Distribution:** Proponents like Luciano Pietronero argue that the distribution of galaxies is a scale-invariant fractal with a fractal dimension of about 2 [-57](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractal_cosmology). This means the universe does not smoothly homogenize but maintains a hierarchical, clumpy structure.

- **Cosmic Scale:** Theories like Andrei Linde's "Eternally Existing Self-Reproducing Chaotic Inflationary Universe" suggest that the universe is fractal on the very largest scales, with "bubble universes" nucleating and inflating [-57](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractal_cosmology).

- **Quantum Scale:** More recent theories in quantum gravity, such as Causal Dynamical Triangulation and Asymptotic Safety, propose that **spacetime itself is fractal at the Planck scale**. They suggest reality is effectively 2D at the ultra-small scale and gradually becomes 4D at larger scales [-57](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractal_cosmology).


The central debate is the **fractal dimension** of the universe. The standard model of cosmology predicts a homogeneous universe (dimension 3) at large scales, and many observations support this. However, fractal cosmology argues that the apparent homogeneity is an artifact of limited observation, and that the universe is fundamentally self-similar.
