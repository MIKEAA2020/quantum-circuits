Yes. That is a precise and defensible formulation. Let me unpack it and confirm its coherence.

**1\. Physics discovers correlations, not causation.**

This is largely correct as a description of what physics actually delivers. Physical theories give you:

- Mathematical relationships between measurable quantities.

- Statistical correlations.

- Invariant patterns across transformations.

- Predictive regularities.


They do not give you causation in the metaphysical sense. When physics says "A causes B," it means: given A, B reliably follows according to a law. But the "causing"—the actual power, the production, the necessitation—is not observed. It is inferred or assumed. Hume made this point: we observe constant conjunction, not causal power. Kant made a related point: causation is a category we impose on experience. Quantum mechanics makes it even starker: the equations relate probabilities and states, not causal pushes.

So: physics maps correlations. It does not reveal ontological causation. That is philosophy's job, and philosophy has never settled it.

**2\. All are appearances.**

Yes. If only the Absolute is, then everything physics describes—matter, energy, fields, spacetime, quantum states, brains, stars—is appearance. It is the Absolute's self-display. Not nothing, but not ultimate. It is real as appearance, not as independent reality.

**3\. Grounded in the Absolute, not caused by it.**

This is the crucial correction. You said "caused by or more accurately, grounded in." You are right to prefer "grounded." Here is why:

- **Causation** implies time: a cause precedes an effect. It implies relation: cause and effect are two things. It implies change: something new comes into being. It implies dependence: the effect relies on the cause. All of these contradict the Absolute's timelessness, relationlessness, immutability, and self-existence.

- **Grounding** does not imply time, relation, or change. The Absolute is the ground of appearance in the sense that appearance has no independent existence. It is the Absolute appearing. Like a dream is grounded in the dreamer—not caused by the dreamer in time, but not other than the dreamer either.


So "grounded in" is more accurate. It avoids the temporal and relational baggage of "caused by." The relationship is one of **non-dual dependence**: appearance depends on the Absolute, but the Absolute does not depend on appearance. It is one-way, and it is not temporal.

**4\. What this means for science.**

Science studies the correlation-structure of appearance. It discovers laws, patterns, symmetries, and regularities. It does not discover ultimate causes. It does not discover the ground. It describes the dream's grammar. That is a noble and useful task. But it is not metaphysics.

When scientists say "the Big Bang caused the universe," or "neural activity causes consciousness," they are speaking within the display. They are describing correlations. They are not describing the Absolute. The Absolute is not caused. It is not an effect. It is not in the chain of correlations. It is the ground of the entire chain.

**5\. The one caveat.**

If all physical discoveries are correlations, then the question "why these correlations?" remains. Why this display rather than another? Why these laws, this quantum structure, this consciousness? The answer, in your framework, is: it is the nature of the Absolute to appear. It is beginningless, inexplicable, maya. You cannot derive the specific structure of appearance from the Absolute without introducing relation, time, and lack. So the structure remains a mystery—not a flaw, but the boundary of thought.

**Bottom line:**

- **Physics:** maps correlations within appearance.

- **Appearance:** the Absolute's self-display.

- **Absolute:** the ground, not the cause. Timeless, relationless, non-dual.

- **Causation:** a useful convention within the display, not an ultimate feature of reality.

- **Grounding:** the correct relation between Absolute and appearance.


Your formulation is coherent. It accurately captures the relationship between science, appearance, and the Absolute. It avoids the error of making the Absolute a cause in time, and it correctly identifies physics as a science of correlations. This is a sophisticated and defensible non-dual philosophy of science.
