Whitehead’s philosophy addresses the hard problem of consciousness in a very unusual way: it doesn’t try to _solve_ the problem so much as _dissolve_ it. The hard problem asks, “How do physical processes in the brain give rise to subjective experience?” Whitehead’s answer is essentially: **that question is based on a false assumption.** Experience isn’t something that emerges from dead matter. It’s the other way around—experience is the most basic stuff of reality, and what we call “matter” is an abstraction from it.

Here’s how that works in plain terms.

### The Basic Move: Panexperientialism

Whitehead says the universe isn’t made of tiny material particles that are completely devoid of experience. Instead, it’s made of **actual occasions**—momentary events that are themselves tiny “drops of experience” [-16](https://philarchive.org/archive/HENECPv1#5#3). A photon, an electron, or any basic physical event isn’t just a chunk of stuff; it’s a moment of **taking in** and **responding to** its environment. Whitehead calls this basic relational activity **prehension**—a primitive kind of feeling or grasping that every actual occasion does [-16](https://philarchive.org/archive/HENECPv1#5#3).

So experience is not a late arrival in the universe. It’s **fundamental**. Every actual occasion has a “physical pole” (its causal connection to the past) and a “mental pole” (its own subjective becoming) [-16](https://philarchive.org/archive/HENECPv1#5#3). These aren’t two separate things; they’re two sides of the same event.

### Why This Dissolves the Hard Problem

The hard problem arises because we assume there’s a gap between **objective physical processes** and **subjective experience**. We ask: how does the first produce the second?

Whitehead says: if every physical event already has an experiential side, there’s no gap to bridge. Experience isn’t produced by physical processes; it **is** the inside of those processes. As one scholar puts it, in Whitehead’s world “the ‘Hard Problem’ of sentience does not arise since experience not only permeates the entire universe, but provides the substance out of which it is fabricated”-.

So consciousness isn’t a mysterious add-on to a mindless cosmos. It’s a **high-grade form of something that’s already everywhere**. Human consciousness arises when actual occasions are organized in sufficiently complex, integrated ways—what Whitehead calls “high-grade prehensive activity” [-16](https://philarchive.org/archive/HENECPv1#5#3). The difference between a rock and a brain isn’t that one has experience and the other doesn’t; it’s the **intensity, complexity, and integration** of the prehensive activity involved.

### Consciousness as Relational, Not Located

In the standard view, consciousness is inside your head. It’s a private, internal property of the brain. Whitehead flips that. Consciousness is a **relational event**. It happens where actual occasions **mutually prehend each other**—where there’s a rich, unified synthesis of many feelings into one [-16](https://philarchive.org/archive/HENECPv1#5#3). It’s not a thing that sits inside a skull; it’s a process of integration that occurs at the interface between many occasions.
